# Geohydrological Analysis Report: Well Selection and Site Evaluation

## 1. Executive Summary
This report evaluates ten potential well sites based on geological cross-sections (Figure 16). The assessment focuses on the yield potential, water quality risk, and long-term reliability of wells located in different stratigraphic units: granite, schist, marble, and alluvium.

---

## 2. Selected and Recommended Sites

### High Yield & High Reliability
*   **Site 5 (Public Supply - Marble near Fault):**
    *   **Reasoning:** Located in fresh marble (Unit I) and strategically placed near a **fault zone**. Marble is susceptible to dissolution, creating secondary porosity. The fault zone acts as a major conduit for groundwater flow, making this the most favorable location for high-capacity public supply.
*   **Site 4 (Irrigation - Fresh Marble):**
    *   **Reasoning:** Penetrates deep into the fresh marble unit. The inherent secondary porosity of carbonate rocks typically supports the high-volume extraction required for irrigation.
*   **Site 10 (Industry - Alluvium near Stream):**
    *   **Reasoning:** Located in alluvium (Unit J) adjacent to a perennial stream. This ensures **induced infiltration** from the stream, providing a high and consistent yield suitable for industrial cooling or processing.

### Moderate Yield & Reliable (Domestic/Farm)
*   **Site 9 (Domestic - Alluvium):**
    *   **Reasoning:** Alluvial deposits of sand and gravel generally have high permeability and storage. This is a reliable source for domestic use with relatively simple well construction.
*   **Site 1 & 8 (Farm - Weathered Zones):**
    *   **Reasoning:** These wells utilize the weathered zones of schist (Unit E) and granite (Unit B). The "saprolite" or weathered rock typically has much higher storage capacity than the underlying fresh rock, making it suitable for moderate farm and stock requirements.

---

## 3. Rejected or Not Recommended Sites

### Low Yield Potential
*   **Site 3 (Domestic - Fresh Schist):**
    *   **Reasoning:** This well penetrates deep into fresh schist (Unit F). Crystalline rocks like schist have negligible primary porosity. Unless a significant fracture is intersected (which is not indicated here), the yield is likely to be extremely low and insufficient for modern domestic needs.
*   **Site 6 (Industry - Fresh Granite):**
    *   **Reasoning:** Similar to Site 3, fresh granite (Unit C) is a poor aquifer. Attempting to draw industrial-scale volumes from fresh, unfractured granite is high-risk and likely to result in well failure.

### Low Reliability / High Vulnerability
*   **Site 2 & 7 (Stock - Residual Soil):**
    *   **Reasoning:** These are very shallow wells completed in the residual soils (Units D and A). They are highly susceptible to:
        1.  **Seasonal Drying:** The water table may drop below the well bottom during droughts.
        2.  **Contamination:** Shallow wells lack the protection of overlying confining layers or depth, making them vulnerable to surface runoff and pollutants.
    *   **Recommendation:** While okay for minimal stock use, they should not be relied upon for critical supply.

---

## 4. Conclusion and Recommendations
For high-demand applications (Public, Industry, Irrigation), priority should be given to **Sites 5, 4, and 10**. For residential development, **Site 9** is the preferred choice. Future exploration should include geophysical surveys to map the exact extent of the fault zone near Site 5 to maximize yield.
